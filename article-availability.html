Availability Sets & Zones, explained from scratch

Keep workloads resilient by spreading VMs across fault domains and physically separate datacenters.

What is Availability?

Availability means your application stays online even when infrastructure fails. Azure offers two tools: Availability Sets (protect against datacenter-level outages) and Availability Zones (protect against entire zone failures). Using either ensures your VMs are never all knocked offline by a single failure point.

Availability Sets

An Availability Set spreads your VMs across multiple physical servers and racks inside a datacenter. Azure groups them into fault domains (independent racks) and update domains (servers rebooted together during updates). Deploy 2-3 VMs in one Availability Set, and Azure guarantees at least one stays running.

Availability Zones

An Availability Zone is a physically separate datacenter with its own power, cooling, and networking. When you deploy VMs across zones (zone 1, zone 2, zone 3), Azure ensures they can survive even a full zone outage. Zone-redundant services automatically replicate across all zones in a region.

Comparing the Two

Availability SetAvailability Zone
Same datacenterDifferent physical datacenters
Protects rack failuresProtects zone-wide outages
No latency penaltyMinimal latency (same region)
FreeFree (data transfer billed)

Getting Started

For Availability Sets: create the set in the Portal or CLI, then add VMs to it. For Availability Zones: when creating a VM, choose a zone (1, 2, or 3). Deploy critical VMs across all three zones for maximum resilience. Combine both: put zone-1 VMs in an Availability Set for extra protection.
