VPN & ExpressRoute, explained from scratch

Connect on-premises networks to Azure securely over the internet or a private circuit.

Site-to-Site VPN

A Site-to-Site VPN encrypts traffic over the public internet, linking your office network to your Azure VNet. You install a VPN device at your office (or use Azure VPN Gateway on both ends), and they establish a secure tunnel. Traffic between your office and Azure is encrypted; anyone sniffing the internet sees garbage.

Point-to-Site VPN

Point-to-Site is for individual users working from home. Install a VPN client on your laptop; it connects to Azure VPN Gateway. Your laptop gets an IP in the VNet and can access resources as if you were in the office. Perfect for remote workers or developers needing secure access.

ExpressRoute

ExpressRoute is a private circuit from your datacenter to Azure, leased from a carrier (not over the public internet). Advantages: higher bandwidth (up to 100 Gbps), lower latency, no internet congestion, and Microsoft doesn't see your traffic. Downside: expensive, requires a carrier relationship. Best for large enterprises with strict compliance needs.

VPN Gateway Architecture

In Azure, you create a VPN Gateway (sits in a special subnet in your VNet). It handles all encryption/decryption and routing. Configure connections to your on-prem VPN device or ExpressRoute circuit. The gateway continuously pings your remote endpoint to ensure the tunnel stays up. If it fails, it can failover to a standby gateway.

Getting Started

For Site-to-Site: Create a VPN Gateway (choose SKU based on throughput). Configure a Local Network Gateway pointing to your office router. Define a connection linking the two. For Point-to-Site: same gateway, but add VPN clients and download a client package. For ExpressRoute: contact a carrier, request a circuit, link it to your VNet via ExpressRoute Gateway. Allow weeks for setup.
